01

Get started

From install to your first replay

  1. 01

    Two permissions

    The goal of this step: hand clicktap the two keys — "may tap for you" and "may float above other apps".

    1. Install clicktap (Android 10+; text recognition needs Android 11+). Read and accept the privacy notice on first launch.
    2. On the home screen, tap the "Accessibility service" row under Runtime status; find "clicktap accessibility service" in the system list and enable it — it is what actually performs taps and gestures.
    3. Then tap "Overlay permission" and allow "display over other apps" — the floating recorder needs it.
    4. Permissions can only be switched in system settings; the rows in the app are doors to them, and status refreshes when you return. Three optional permissions, each for its feature: notification access (message trigger), alarms & reminders (punctual schedules), battery optimization exemption (survives background limits).

    Checkpoint： Back in clicktap, both rows read green "on" and the "Start recording" button lights up.

  2. 02

    Record it, then replay it

    The goal of this step: turn a stretch of real actions into a task, then replay it once yourself.

    Record

    1. Tap "Start recording" on the home screen and name the task, say "Daily check-in". The name is a label only; it does not affect replay.
    2. A floating strip appears at the screen edge, still standing by. Recording starts only when you tap "Start recording" on the strip.
    3. Use the target app like always: every tap, long-press and swipe is recorded and performed. The real pause between two moves becomes the next action's wait time (capped at 60 seconds).
    4. Need Back or Home? Use the strip's buttons — the system navigation keys cannot be recorded.
    5. "Pause" can wait; "Finish" saves the task. The strip turns red while recording, orange while paused.

    Replay

    1. Tap the task on the home screen to open its details, then tap "Run replay".
    2. The replay is armed first; it actually starts when you tap "Start" on the overlay.
    3. While it plays you can pause, resume, or go "step by step" — one action at a time, to check each target.

    Checkpoint： The task details show the recorded actions, and a replay runs from start to finish.

  3. 03

    Multi-finger gestures

    The goal of this step: pinch-to-zoom and other multi-finger moves can be recorded too.

    While recording, hold the screen with two or more fingers and draw the gesture; when the last finger lifts, it is saved — up to three fingers. Gesture traces come from recording and cannot be edited finger by finger — re-record instead. If the gesture needs a clean screen, record it on a blank one.

    Checkpoint：The recorded gesture action replays with the multi-finger trace intact.

  4. 04

    Help every action find its target

    The goal of this step: understand targeting — it decides whether the task still works weeks later.

    1. Tap any action in the task details to open the action editor.
    2. Control lookup (most reliable): match by control ID, visible text or description, combined with and/or; the best way is tapping the control on the real screen and letting clicktap read it. Text matches by containment, so small copy changes still hit.
    3. Screen position (fallback): coordinates are stored as screen percentages and survive different resolutions; tap "Open app to pick coordinates" and point on the frozen frame. It cannot judge screen state — fallback only.
    4. Text recognition (Android 11+): when a control has no ID (canvas, web, games), frame up to three regions and search on-screen text; the tap lands on the matched text's center.
    5. Every action also has a wait timeout: 0 = execute immediately; positive = wait for the target to appear. That is what beats splash ads — there is a whole scenario below.

    Checkpoint：The key action uses control lookup with a wait, not bare coordinates.

  5. 05

    Loops and the flowchart

    The goal of this step: circle a repeating stretch and let the app do the laps.

    1. Switch the task details to the flowchart view.
    2. Long-press an action and choose "Set as loop start" from the menu, then pick a later action as the end.
    3. In the dialog, set the repeat count and what happens when an action inside the loop misses its target: abort the replay, or drop this round and continue. Loops nest, but a loop is a contiguous run, so loops cannot cross.

    Checkpoint：The loop range shows an "×N" badge on the flowchart, and a replay runs the laps.

  6. 06

    Let it start itself

    The goal of this step: the task runs at the right moment without you.

    1. Task details → "Auto start". One task, one trigger at a time; manual runs always work.
    2. Scheduled trigger: pick weekdays and times; pick the starting point — go home first (recommended), open the target app, or replay on screen. Due while locked, the device is woken with a reminder (vibration, ringtone, or your own sound); the run continues after unlock.
    3. Message trigger: runs when a notification matches keywords — a full scenario below.
    4. App launch: runs when a chosen app comes to the foreground.
    5. A schedule is only as punctual as the background — do the vendor background settings in "Common pitfalls" first.

    Checkpoint：After saving a trigger, the task card shows it, and the task runs on time by itself.

Scenarios

Put the skills to work on real tasks

Scenario: skipping the same splash ad for the hundredth time

Every launch of some app means seconds of ad and a careful tap on "Skip" — let clicktap tap it.

Before you start

Main steps 01–02 done: you can record and replay.

Steps

  1. Record: open the app, wait for the ad, tap "Skip", finish and name the task.
  2. In the task details, open the "Skip" action and switch targeting to control lookup with the text "Skip"; no usable ID? Use text recognition around the button area.
  3. Give that action a wait timeout of 8000 ms to start: the wait is "keep looking at most this long" — the tap fires the instant the button appears, no dead waiting on short ads.
  4. In "Auto start", pick "App launch" and choose this app — from now on it is skipped the moment the app opens.

Why not a fixed delay: ad length varies every time, and a fixed 5 seconds usually wakes after the ad is gone and taps nothing. Waiting for the target to actually appear survives that.

Verify

Checkpoint：Reopen the app: the ad button is tapped the moment it appears, hands-free.

Scenario: importing someone's Playbook

Every entry in the Playbook community is a task package plus a title and a description. A task someone else tuned works on your device after one import.

Before you start

None — a freshly installed clicktap can import.

Steps

  1. Open the Playbook page in your phone's browser and tap "Download" — the file is handed straight to clicktap, which opens its import screen. Opening a .clicktap file from a file manager works too.
  2. The dialog lists the tasks inside; tick the ones you want.
  3. If a name collides with one of yours, choose to overwrite it or save as a copy.
  4. Imported tasks have their trigger switched off — they will not start on their own. Read the actions first, then run it manually.

gkd subscription rules (JSON5, by URL or file) import as well, then run as always-on matching you can toggle per subscription. Your own tasks export as .clicktap from the share icon: save locally, share via the system sheet, or "Share to the website" after signing in with GitHub. Publishing stores the package as-is — notes, matched texts and keywords all ride along, so confirm the disclosure list item by item.

Verify

Checkpoint：The imported task is in your list; it carries no trigger and will not start by itself.

Scenario: the notification arrives, the task is already moving

Some chances vanish in seconds: red-packet-style notifications are gone before you switch apps, limited coupons expire in minutes. The message trigger starts a task the instant the notification lands.

Before you start

Enable the "notification access" permission in system settings.

Steps

  1. Task details → "Auto start" → "Message trigger".
  2. Pick the source app and enter keywords: the title or body matching any keyword fires the task.
  3. Tick "open the notification first" so the replay starts on the right page.

Two reminders

If the source app hides message previews, clicktap only sees placeholder text like "1 new message" and your keywords never match — enable previews for that app. And automate only what you are entitled to automate, respecting the target app's terms of service.

Verify

Checkpoint：Send a test notification with the keyword: the task starts itself, hands-free.

Going further

When something misbehaves, start here

When something breaks, read the execution log

The log answers both questions you actually have: why did this not run (a permission got switched off? a condition never matched?), and which action failed. Records are grouped by run, with segments split after long silences. Debug mode also overlays the gesture trace during replays — the fastest way to see where a tap really landed.

When a task suddenly stops working, it is almost always one of these, in this order:

  1. The target app changed its interface. Find the failed action in the log and re-target it. Control IDs survive redesigns better than text.
  2. The screen was not where you expected. Give the action before the failure a longer wait, or set the starting point to "go home first".
  3. A system update switched a permission off. Check Runtime status on the home screen.
  4. A dialog appeared that was not there when you recorded. Record the dismissal too, targeted by ID or text.

Common pitfalls: not bugs

The vendor kills the background

Several Chinese Android systems freeze background apps shortly after the screen goes off — even a foreground service cannot stop it. The symptom: "the scheduled run never fired". In Settings → Keep-alive: enable battery optimization exemption; check the background-guard status (OK / needs attention / at risk, with "try to revive"); and walk through the four vendor background steps — only you can do these:

  1. In the recents screen, pull down the clicktap card and lock it;
  2. Phone manager → App launch management → turn off "automatic management", enable self-start, associated start and background activity;
  3. Battery optimization → clicktap → choose "not allowed";
  4. Turn off power-saving mode.

The accessibility service gets switched off

System updates or aggressive power saving may silently disable the accessibility service. The guard notification tells you; a red row on the home screen is the other signal. Tap it and re-enable.

Text recognition stops working

When the app says the accessibility service has no screenshot capability, go to system settings andswitch the clicktap accessibility service off and back on once. Common after an app update or reinstall.

Schedules fire late

Without the "alarms & reminders" special permission, the system may postpone scheduled runs. A task due while locked does not execute immediately: it posts a reminder and continues after unlock. On Android 14+, lighting the screen from lock also needs the full-screen notification (alarms & reminders) permission; without it the reminder still rings and vibrates.
