Android · Accessibility-driven automation

Record it once. Replay it whenever.

clicktap records the taps you already make in any app, saves them as a task, and replays that task automatically — on a schedule, when a message arrives, or the moment an app opens.

What it does

Built for the routines you repeat: daily check-ins, sign-ins, claim flows, skipping the same splash ad for the hundredth time.

Record a routine

A floating bar records your clicks, long-presses, swipes and multi-finger gestures while the app underneath still responds. Pause or finish from the bar at any time.

Start it automatically

Run a task at a set time on chosen weekdays, when a chosen app posts a notification matching your keywords, or when a chosen app comes to the foreground. Manual replay always works too.

Target controls, not pixels

Point at a control by resource ID, text or content description — alone or as an AND/OR condition chain — and pick it straight off the real screen. Coordinates are a fallback, and they are stored as screen ratios so a different phone still works.

Find text with OCR

When a control has no usable ID, search for on-screen text instead, inside up to three framed regions. Latin and Chinese recognition, Android 11 and above.

Wait for the page, not a fixed sleep

A step can wait until its target actually appears before tapping. This is what makes splash ads work: a fixed delay usually expires after the ad is already gone.

Loops and a flow chart

Group steps into loops that repeat up to five times, nest them, and read the whole task as a flow chart instead of a list.

Know why it failed

One execution log answers both questions: why a run did not start, and which step failed when one did. Record every step for detail, or filter straight to failures.

Multi-finger gestures

Draw two- or three-finger gestures on a blank screen and the real path is captured, not an approximation.

Your tasks are portable

Export any task or all of them as a .clicktap backup and restore it on another device — or share it to the playbook. gkd-format subscription rules can be imported too.

Nothing leaves your phone

Recording, replay, task storage and logs are all local. There is no account, no analytics and no upload. The app only asks for the permissions each feature actually needs, and explains every one of them.

How it works

Three steps, once per routine.

  1. 01

    Grant two permissions

    Accessibility (so clicks and gestures can be performed) and overlay (so the recorder bar can be shown). Notification access and alarms are optional, and only needed by the matching features.

  2. 02

    Record the routine

    Tap "Start recording", then just use the app the way you normally would. Each tap becomes a step you can edit, retarget or annotate afterwards.

  3. 03

    Replay it

    Run it manually whenever you like, or attach a trigger so it runs itself. Decide whether replay should start from the home screen, from the target app, or from wherever you are.
