Privacy Policy

2026-09-14

Effective date: 2026-09-14 · Last updated: 2026-09-14

This policy explains what clicktap and this website collect, why, and what you can do about it. It covers two things: the clicktap Android application (the App) and this website, including its public task-sharing area (the Site). Both are provided by Beijing Fenghong Technology Co., Ltd..

1. Information We Collect

1.1 In the App

The App has no account system and does not upload your data. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK and no crash-reporting SDK.

  • What you record stays on your device. Tasks, action lists, loop structures, auto-start settings, execution logs, imported rule subscriptions and exported backups are all stored in the App's private storage on your phone.
  • Accessibility data is read, not transmitted. To perform the actions you recorded, the App reads accessibility node information — control text, control identifiers, content descriptions and coordinates — from the app you are automating. That information is used at that moment to find the right control and is not sent anywhere. What the App doesstore locally is what your task needs in order to work later: the target you chose for each step, such as a control ID, a piece of text, or a screen position.
  • Screenshots. If a step uses text recognition, the App asks Android for a screenshot to recognise text in the region you framed. Screenshots are processed in memory for that step. They are not saved to your gallery and not uploaded.
  • Notification text. If you enable a "Message" trigger, the App reads the title and body of notifications from the apps you selected, in order to check your keywords.
  • Installed apps. The App lists installed apps so you can pick a target. The list is read on your device and is not exported in your backups.

1.2 On the Site

The Site collects only what it needs to run the sharing area:

  • Your GitHub identity, if you sign in. We receive your GitHub user ID, login name, display name and avatar URL, and we store them to attribute what you publish. We request only the read:user scope; we cannot read your repositories, and we never receive your GitHub password.
  • Anything you publish. If you publish a task package, we store the file itself, the title and description you write, an optional cover image, and technical details derived from the file (task name, number of steps, whether it has an auto-start trigger, the recording resolution, and the package names of the apps it targets). Published items are visible to everyone.
  • Reports you file. If you report a published item, we store the report, your account, and the reason you gave.
  • Download counts. We count downloads of published items. The counter is aggregate and is not linked to who downloaded.
  • No cookies for tracking. The Site sets no advertising or analytics cookies. Local storage in your browser is used for one thing only: remembering your language choice. Signing in necessarily stores a session token in your browser.

2. How We Use Information

  • To operate and display the sharing area, and to attribute published items to their author.
  • To let others download a package you chose to publish.
  • To handle reports and remove content that breaks the rules.
  • To keep the service working, diagnose faults, and prevent abuse.

We do not sell your information, and we do not use it for advertising or profiling. We do not use your activity to train machine-learning models.

3. Permissions The App Requests

  • Accessibility service — required. It is what lets the App perform clicks and gestures, and read the controls needed to find them. Without it, replay cannot work.
  • Display over other apps — required for the floating recorder bar and the capture layer used while recording.
  • Notification access — optional. Used only by "Message" triggers, to read notification text from apps you select.
  • Alarms & reminders, and full-screen notifications — optional. Used so scheduled runs fire on time and so a locked-screen reminder can wake the display.
  • Battery optimisation exemption — optional, to reduce the chance that Android stops a run in the background.
  • Internet — used only when you choose to import rules from a URL.

Every permission is explained in the App before you grant it, and each one can be revoked in Android settings at any time.

4. Storage, Security and Retention

  • App data stays in the App's private storage and is removed when you uninstall the App.
  • Site data is stored with our hosting providers (see section 5). Task packages are held in private storage and are served through short-lived signed links that stop working when an item is removed.
  • Published items stay published until you delete them or we remove them. Deleting an item removes it from the Site and stops new downloads; it cannot recall copies that were already downloaded.
  • We use HTTPS everywhere and restrict database access to the minimum each operation needs. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

5. Third-Party Services

These are the only third parties involved. We do not use advertising or tracking services.

  • Google ML Kit text recognition (App, optional feature). When a step uses text recognition, the App uses Google ML Kit's on-device text recognition models, in the Latin and Chinese variants. The models are bundled with the App and recognition runs on your device; text and images are not sent to Google. ML Kit is a Google product and is subject to Google's terms. If you never use a text-recognition step, this component is not used.Note: earlier versions of this policy stated that the App integrated no third-party SDK that collects user information. That statement was written before text recognition shipped and was not accurate afterwards. It is corrected here.
  • Supabase (Site). Provides the Site's sign-in, database and file storage. Our project is hosted in the region shown in your Supabase project settings. Supabase processes this data on our behalf.
  • GitHub (Site sign-in, and source hosting). Used to sign you in and to host the App's source code and releases. GitHub's own privacy policy governs your GitHub account.

6. What Publishing Makes Public

This section matters more than any other on this page, so it is stated plainly.

A task package is a faithful copy of what you recorded. When you publish one, it is stored and published exactly as it is, with nothing removed. Depending on how you recorded it, a published package can contain:

  • the task name, and any note you typed on individual steps;
  • the on-screen text, content descriptions and control IDs your steps look for — which can include contact names, message snippets, prices or account labels visible on your screen;
  • the keywords a message trigger waits for in notifications;
  • the package names and app names of every app the task touches;
  • internal screen class names of the target app;
  • the screen resolution you recorded on, and timing and identifier values;
  • and, if your task uses a custom reminder sound, the sound file itself, copied verbatim, along with its original filename.

Before publishing, the Site shows you an itemised list of exactly what it found in your package, with the real values. Read it. Only publish a package that contains nothing you would not hand to a stranger, and do not publish other people's personal information. You can also turn offInclude the embedded reminder sound, which removes the sound file and its filename reference before publishing.

Once a package has been downloaded by someone else, we cannot take it back from them.

7. Your Choices and Rights

  • Delete a published item at any time from its page or from the link in your account area. It is removed for everyone and further downloads stop.
  • Sign out at any time; the session token is discarded from your browser.
  • Request access, correction or deletion of the account data we hold about you (your GitHub identity and anything you published) by writing tosupport@company.example.com. We will respond within 30 days.
  • If you are in a jurisdiction with specific data-protection rights, those rights apply to the data described above, and you may also complain to your local supervisory authority.

8. Children

The App and the Site are not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your jurisdiction), and we do not knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child has published something, contact us and we will remove it.

9. International Transfers

Our service providers may process data in countries other than yours. Where required, we rely on the safeguards those providers offer for international transfers. If you publish personal information about someone else, you are responsible for having a lawful basis to do so.

10. Changes to This Policy

We may update this policy as the App and the Site change. The effective date above always reflects the current version, and material changes will be noted in the App or on the Site. The previous version of this policy covered the App only and claimed no third-party SDKs; it has been superseded by this document.
